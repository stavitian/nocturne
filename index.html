CHAPTER I · 23:00

The first hour belongs to vapor

The nebula is not burning — it is remembering. Ancient light scattered through dust until it learned to glow in colors no eye named for centuries.

  • Filaments — ionized veils a hundred light-years wide
  • Palette — hydrogen rose, oxygen teal, starlight cream
CHAPTER II · 01:00

The second hour is gravity's hour

Every falling star is small debris meeting air at eleven miles per second. What you wish on is a grain of a comet, signing its name in fire across eighty miles of sky.

  • Velocity — ~11–72 km/s, gone in under a second
  • Rhythm — the quiet minutes between are part of the show
CHAPTER III · 03:00

The last hour is yours

By three the horizon has turned over without moving. The same stars, a different sky. Astronomers call it seeing; insomniacs call it company.
